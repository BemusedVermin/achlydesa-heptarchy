# M1-17.F2 · Advance the timeline to the player's next real action

## Goal

Control returns to the player only when they have at least one offered option other than waiting, so no one ever presses wait repeatedly to get through a step's recovery.

## Context

- Found while playing the TUI sample game (`go tool task run -- play --seed 1`), then confirmed by an agent with a headless repro on seed 1 and real content. The owner's requirement: the timeline must progress to the point where the player can take another action; pressing wait over and over is not acceptable.
- M1-17 decides readiness by free usable channels (`internal/core/timeline/timeline.go:177-205`, `decisionTick`). M1-20's usable hook (`internal/core/actions/choices.go:126`, `:177-180`) marks a channel usable when some action *could* start with it, with no check that the action has a valid target.
- After M2-08 and M2-09 gave the party arm actions (strike, pin, sever, give), a free arm counts as usable, so each party body is "ready" on every tick.
- `action.step` needs the `body` channel (`content/data/actions.cue:10-19`), busy for `step_ticks` (10, 15 or 25; `content/data/timeline.cue:9`) via `timeline.Extend` (`internal/core/explore/install.go:209-213`). While it is busy, `actions/options.go:40-43` drops the step options silently.
- Result: after one step the player gets control back after 1 tick with no step option, and needs about 10 waits (`.`) per step. Both party members are affected. Terrain, hidden blockers and leader switching were ruled out.
- Why tests missed it: `internal/frontend/tui/render_test.go:30-37` strips the party to `action.step` only, so the body is the only usable channel and one wait jumps straight past the recovery.

## Deliverables

- `internal/core/timeline` and/or `internal/core/actions`: a readiness rule under which a player-controlled body gets control only at a tick where it has an offered option other than wait.
- A test in `internal/core/game` or `internal/frontend/tui` with real content (not the stripped party) that steps twice and asserts control comes back only when a step, or another real action, is offered, with no waits in between.
- `docs/` updated if the timeline or actions text describes readiness differently.

## Spec

Suggested, not firm. The rule: a body decides at the earliest tick where some channel is free *and* an action could start there with a valid target. In outline:

1. When a body is player-controlled and its only offered options are wait, `Run` keeps advancing to the next tick where an option appears, or an event that interrupts (an alert, a hit, a landing).
2. Wait stays available and stays meaningful, for example to let a pinned enemy act. An option that exists but is disabled for a reason does not count as offered.
3. Prefer one readiness rule in the core over a TUI loop that skips ahead, so the Ebitengine frontend gets it too (AGENTS.md rule 7).
4. Optional and separate: show a step blocked by recovery as a disabled option with a reason such as "still recovering", rather than dropping it silently. Settle whether that is wanted before building it.

Mind the interaction with M2-07's idle-body handling and with combat pacing: combat must still give the player the first free arm or leg at the tick it frees, so check the lethality targets from M2-11 after the change.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] With the real sample content on seed 1, `d` then `d` again needs no wait: the second step is offered the moment control returns.
- [ ] The new real-content test fails before the fix and passes after.
- [ ] In a fight, a player whose arm is free and has a valid target still gets control at that tick (existing combat goldens unchanged, or changes explained in Notes).
- [ ] `go tool task check` passes.
