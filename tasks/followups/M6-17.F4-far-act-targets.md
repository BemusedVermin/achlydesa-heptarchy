# M6-17.F4 · Give far agents' take and put a target when the plan names none

## Goal

A far agent's take or put lands on the source or container it reached, as the near picker would choose it.

## Context

- `internal/core/agents/lod.go`, `act`: a far step other than travel or wander applies its action's `on_start` and `on_land` effects at once, aimed at the step's target, or at the agent itself when the step has none. M6-13's take and put pickers choose the nearest believed source or container when the step names none; far, that choice is not made, so such a step would aim at the agent.
- `act` also skips the action's `requires` formula and its range, as a coarse operator does.

## Deliverables

- `internal/core/agents`: a far choice of target for steps whose picker picks one (the nearest believed landmark in the agent's chunk with the item, by belief, never by sight), registered beside the picker.

## Spec

- **Firm:** far agents never call sight or cell pathing.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] A far agent takes water from a well it reached and drinks it, with M6-13's operators.
- [x] `go tool task check` passes.

## Out of scope

- Near take and put: M6-13.

## Notes

Triaged 2026-11-27: todo in M6.

- The far choice is an optional `FarPicker` interface (`PickFar`) a registered picker may implement, as `Finisher` is, rather than a second registry. `act` uses it when the operator's picker has one, and otherwise aims as before (the step's target, else the agent).
- `thingPicker.PickFar` aims at the step's named landmark, or the nearest one `Known` lists for the operator's near fact, when the agent believes it stands in its own chunk. A named target is now held to that chunk too, by the step's `Target.At`.
- `consumePicker` also has `PickFar` (its `Pick` reads only the agent's inventory): far `act` passed no choices before, so a far consume could never land.

- Docs: the Far agents bullet in `docs/go-technical-design.md` now says how a far step is aimed.
