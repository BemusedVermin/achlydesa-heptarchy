# M1-10.F1.F1 · Plan view: draw by reachability within a limit

## Goal

The plan view shows where the party can go: accessible means reachable, computed by `hexmap` pathing from the focus within a range limit, with elevation shown by tone (`Below`). This replaces the depth window M1-10.F1 added.

## Context

- M1-10.F1 added `party.plan_floor_depth` (4 for now). In `nearest` in `internal/core/explore/project.go`, a hex's wall candidates are ignored when a standable cell of that hex lies within that many depths of the focus, above or below.
- That is a depth window around the focus. It is not reachability: it never checks whether the party can step to the cell, and `party.max_step_up` and `party.max_step_down` play no part in it.
- Intent as the owner stated it: render the accessible areas, with elevation indicated by tone. M1-10.F1's "slope hexes must render as floors" criterion was read too literally; the window is a stand-in for that intent.
- Cases where the two differ: a standable ledge within 4 depths but unreachable (a shelf above a cliff) is drawn as floor and hides the cliff's wall; a reachable floor more than 4 depths away still loses to nearer walls.

## Deliverables

- `explore/project.go`: compute the reachable set from the focus with `hexmap` pathing, honouring the party's step limits (`max_step_up`, `max_step_down`), capped at a range from content (a new `party` number, tunable). A hex draws its reachable standable cell; walls are drawn only where nothing in the hex is reachable.
- Remove `party.plan_floor_depth` and its schema and load checks, unless the range cap reuses it.
- Tests for both directions of the descent slope, an unreachable ledge, and the range cap.

## Spec

Decided by the owner: reachability by `hexmap` pathing, within a limit (option 2 of the original proposal).

Suggestions, not decisions (the most reversible choices; note any change in Notes):

- The cap is a path cost or hex distance from the focus, from content.
- Seen-but-not-visible cells use the same rule against remembered cells.
- Pathing runs over cells the party has seen or can see, not the whole map.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Reachability comes from `hexmap` pathing, with a content-set range limit.
- [x] Walking `map.test.descent` up and down its slope draws the accessible cells with `Below` changing smoothly, in both directions.
- [x] An unreachable ledge within the old window does not hide the wall below or beside it.
- [x] `go tool task check` passes.

## Out of scope

- Sight and pathing across map links (M1-14.F1).
- Frontend tone choices for `Below`.

## Notes

Filed while answering the owner's review of PR #26.

Done. `buildMap` in `explore/project.go` now asks `hexmap` for the cells the party can walk to from the focus and draws a hex's reachable cell, nearest the focus in depth, ahead of everything else in the hex. Hexes with nothing reachable fall back to the old rule (nearest candidate, shallower on a tie, visible before remembered), so walls show only there. `party.plan_floor_depth` and its checks are gone.

Choices on the Spec's suggestions:

- Cap: a number of steps, `party.plan_range`, 12 to start (a placeholder; the test descent's route from the hall's centre to the chamber is about 10 steps). Steps, not hex distance, because pathing counts steps and a step can change depth as well as hex.
- Pathing runs only over cells the party has seen or sees now (the focus cell is always allowed). A remembered reachable floor is drawn as remembered; visibility of a reachable cell is whether it is in sight now.
- Seen-but-not-visible cells follow the same rule, as one reachable set over both.
- Unit step cost, so the cap is a plain step count. The timeline's ticks and `climb_cost` play no part.

Files touched outside the list: `internal/core/hexmap/path.go` and `path_test.go` (new `Map.Reachable`, a breadth-first flood over `Step`; `Path` is a point-to-point A* and could not give a reachable set), `internal/content/types.go`, `internal/content/load.go`, `internal/content/types_go_gen.cue`, `content/data/party.cue`.

Tests: `TestTheSlopeIsDrawnAsFloorWalkingDownAndBackUp` (both directions), `TestAnUnreachableLedgeDoesNotHideTheWallBesideIt` (a visible shelf two cells up that no step reaches), `TestTheRangeCapLeavesFarSlopeFloorToTheWalls`, `TestReachableWalksTheStepRulesWithinTheLimit`.
