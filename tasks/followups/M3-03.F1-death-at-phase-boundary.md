# M3-03.F1 · Don't crash when a body dies at the phase boundary its own decision crosses

## Goal

`timeline.Run` survives a body that leaves the timeline while the clock advances to that body's own decision, so need thresholds that kill can run in a live game.

## Context

- `internal/core/timeline/timeline.go`, `Run`: after `st.pop` it calls `st.open`, which advances the clock with `clk.Advance`. Advancing crosses phase boundaries, so per-phase systems run, among them `agents.needs`. A need threshold (`need.water` at 0.9 damages the torso) can kill the body, and combat's `leaveTimeline` removes its `comp.Actor`. `Run` then reads `st.actors.Get(body).Controller` (line 427) and dereferences nil.
- Found while writing M3-03's agents tests: a non-protected `template.test.drinker` with `need.water` rising by 0.5 per phase panics in `TestADrinkerWandersThenWalksToWaterOnceThirsty` within 2000 ticks when the sample thresholds are kept. The tests clear the thresholds to stay clear of it.
- It is not specific to agents: any body under any controller that a per-phase or per-day system kills at the boundary its decision crosses hits it. Until M3-03 no body without plot armor carried needs, so the sample game never met it.

## Deliverables

- `internal/core/timeline/timeline.go`: after `st.open`, check that the body is still alive and still has an `Actor` (and isn't otherwise taken off the timeline) before reading its controller; if not, continue the loop.
- A test in `internal/core/timeline` or `internal/core/agents` where a per-phase system kills the body whose decision crosses the boundary, and `Run` carries on.

## Spec

- A body that leaves the timeline during `open` doesn't decide; `Run` goes on to the next entry. Whether `api.Ready` should still be published for it is the implementer's call; say which in Notes.

- Tuning the need thresholds.
